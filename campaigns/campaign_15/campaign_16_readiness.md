# Campaign 15 — Campaign 16 Readiness Assessment (report only)

**Readiness verdict: NOT READY for a cross-family transfer claim;
ready for one focused dynamics experiment.**

## Why not transfer yet

- The self-control — the easiest possible transplant — still ends
  ≈6.8–7.0 against a 2.77 native score. The dominant cause is the
  placement (+9.24 initial damage): deleting blocks 11–12,
  duplicating 24–29, and reordering depth. Testing a foreign
  donor inside a topology that costs +9.24 before the donor says
  anything would repeat Campaigns 7–13's confound with better
  connectors.
- The connector ranking is budget-relative (Campaign 15's central
  finding), so any single-budget transfer readout would be
  uninterpretable without trajectory reporting.

## Recommended single next experiment (Campaign 16 candidate)

A preregistered **optimization-dynamics study on the self-control**
before any donor work:
- Both connector inits × learning rates {1e-4, 3e-4, 1e-3} × seeds
  {42, 123, 31415}, trajectory endpoints at 0/8/16/32/64/96/192,
  full curves published; question: is the step-32 old-connector
  advantage a learning-rate artifact of the zero-init branch, and
  does the new connector's late lead grow with budget?
- In parallel design (not run) a **native-depth-respecting
  placement** for a future cross-family pilot: the donor sector
  replaces recipient blocks at matched depth with no duplication,
  gated by this project's reconstruction tests, with intact vs
  scrambled vs random controls and the same-family reference.

Only after the dynamics question is answered and a placement with
a measured, acceptable damage budget exists should a cross-family
pilot be preregistered. The sealed held-out set stays sealed
until a future promotion gate — defined before that pilot — is
passed.
