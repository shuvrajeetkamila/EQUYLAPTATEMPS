# Campaign 13 — Final Report: The Same-Family Positive Control

**Project:** HETEROGENEOUS AI MODEL SURGERY
**Question:** Can the surgery pipeline preserve learned structure
when donor and recipient are genuinely compatible — same
architecture, same tokenizer family, same lineage (SmolLM-135M
recipient; SmolLM-135M-Instruct donor sector)?
**Classification: Outcome C** — controls valid; the positive
control does **not** pass. **Promotion:** false.
**Held-out:** `NOT_RUN_NO_PROMOTION` (frozen for all outcomes in
this same-family campaign).

## The dominant finding: surgery itself is the bottleneck

Reference points measured in the same jobs: native recipient
macro **2.7731**; native donor **3.0369**; self-reconstruction
child (condition F — the recipient's *own* sector in the graft
slot) **7.4577**; intact positive-control child **7.34–7.56**.
Transplantation through this pipeline at these budgets costs
roughly **+4.7 NLL** no matter whose sector is inserted —
including the model's own. (F is not a literal identity: the
sector is also relocated to a shallower position and the child
carries 34 blocks instead of 30, on top of freshly initialized
seams and 32 training steps.) Against that integration damage,
the learned-arrangement effect being hunted — the base↔Instruct
difference is only 5.64% RMS in the sector weights — is
second-order: at most ~0.45 NLL (seed 123), inconsistent across
seeds, and washed out entirely by 96 steps at seed 42.

## Interpretation (per the frozen framework)

The positive control **fails as specified**: even in the
friendliest possible setting, the pipeline does not reproducibly
preserve the donor sector's learned arrangement at the frozen
evidentiary bar. Descriptively the intact sector is more often
ahead than behind (unlike the cross-family campaigns, where seed
31415 reversed hard) — so the data hint that *some* arrangement
signal may exist here — but hints are not the preregistered
standard, and the honest classification is Outcome C with
`arrangement_sensitivity: none`.

Combined with Campaigns 11–12, the evidence now favors the
second explanation the campaign set out to test: **the surgery
regime itself — frozen graft, small trained seams, 32–96 steps of
tiny-data training — cannot reliably preserve or reveal learned
structure, even when donor and recipient are compatible.**
Cross-family incompatibility remains possible but is no longer
the leading explanation; nothing in these campaigns shows the
cross-family setting performing *worse than* the same-family
setting once host quality is accounted for.

## What would be needed to detect structure (next-step logic)

The measured bottleneck is integration damage (+4.7 NLL), so
future work should attack that first: e.g. identity-preserving
interface initialization (seams initialized so the child starts
near the recipient's own function), longer seam training, or
grafting at the sector's native depth. Any such study needs a new
preregistration; none is authorized here. A cheaper decisive
diagnostic also exists: track the self-reconstruction child (F)
over training budgets — the budget at which F approaches the
native recipient bounds the regime's ability to preserve *any*
structure.

## Limitations

One same-family pair; the donor's learned delta is small (5.64%
RMS) and its direction on this endpoint is adverse natively
(instruct tuning worsens plain NLL), so the detector test is
conservative. Three seeds; seed variance (±0.2–0.45 NLL within a
condition) is comparable to the effects sought. NLL may be
insensitive to the instruct-specific behaviors the donor actually
gained; no chat-format capability evaluation was preregistered.
