# Campaign 11 — Decision Memo

**Date:** 2026-10-14
**Decision:** Classify Campaign 11 as **Outcome C**. Do not promote.
Held-out remains `NOT_RUN_NO_PROMOTION`. Do not begin Campaign 12
automatically.

## Why

The campaign was designed to decide between two explanations of the
Campaign 9–10 donor-specific advantage: (i) the donor's *learned
internal organization* transfers, or (ii) the *statistics and scale*
of pretrained weights, passed through a donor-geometry-aware
interface, produce the effect. The within-tensor permutation control
separates these cleanly — it preserves every per-tensor statistic
exactly while destroying arrangement — and it passed all frozen
validity criteria, including graft activity comparable to the intact
sector (so the null result cannot be blamed on a dead control).

The intact sector's advantage over its scrambled self was +3.62% at
seed 42 but −0.04% at seed 123 and −1.62% at 96 steps. The frozen
primary gate requires both seeds; it fails. Per the preregistered
outcome mapping, that is Outcome C.

## Consequences for the programme

- Campaigns 9–10's "donor-specific benefit" should be described as a
  fragile, seed-dependent effect consistent with weight statistics
  and scale, not as evidence of transferred learned structure.
- The most defensible positive finding remains narrow: a
  donor-geometry-aware interface with a trained output pathway makes
  a cross-family block transplant *work at all* at small budgets —
  but absolute quality stays well below the usefulness ceiling
  (macro ≈7.7–8.7 vs 6.00 required; language ≈1.8× host vs 1.50×).
- Any future campaign claiming learned-structure transfer must beat
  a statistics-preserved control at both seeds by the frozen margins;
  that is now the standing evidentiary bar. Candidate directions
  (not authorized): different placement/depth of the sector, larger
  budgets, or permutation controls at finer granularity (e.g.
  row/column structured permutations) to localize where arrangement
  starts to matter.
