# Campaign 9 Decision Memo (plain English)

**Did the interface hypothesis survive?** Partly — in a specific, narrow
way. When we shaped the connectors to speak the donor's own "geometric
language," the real pretrained slice finally beat its random twin, clearly
and repeatedly (about +10%, twice, plus again with triple training time).
That had never happened outside Campaign 4's much smaller effect.

**Did the pretrained donor beat the random twin?** Yes — in all three
geometry comparisons. **Was it replicated?** Yes, across seeds and budgets.
**Was the donor causally necessary?** Yes — removing or zeroing it badly
hurt those children.

**But did the child meet the quality gates?** No, not close. All geometry
children remained far worse than the host alone (language ~1.8–2.0× worse
vs the 1.5× limit). **Was held-out testing permitted?** No —
`NOT_RUN_NO_PROMOTION`.

**The crucial honesty point:** the geometry connectors mostly *switched
off* the random twin (it became a near-empty pipe), rather than making the
pretrained child better in absolute terms than the old connectors did. So
the strongest supported claim is: *"pretrained donor blocks specifically
can use their native geometry, and that contribution is real and causal —
but it does not yet make a useful child."* That is **Outcome B**, not a
breakthrough claim.

**What should happen next (recommendation, not launched):** one sharper
test — keep the geometry-matched input, but pair it with a fully trained
output pathway *and* a stronger random control that is pre-scaled to stay
"awake" in donor geometry. If the pretrained advantage survives an active
twin and absolute quality climbs toward the gates, the programme has a
real lead; if the advantage was mostly "the twin fell asleep," we will
know that too. Either answer is valuable, and it is a small, cheap
experiment.
